Aluminum language
Aluminum is the credibility layer. It makes the soft parts feel held.

    • Use aluminum as edges, rules, hardware metaphors, dividers, and
      control affordances.
    • Never make the whole interface metallic. The aluminum should be the
      frame, not the room.
    • Digital translation: 1px cool borders, subtle top highlights, low-opacity
      inner shadows, and clear radius consistency.
    • Physical translation: brushed or anodized silver, rounded corners,
      visible clips, hinge logic, and tactile closure details.

MATERIALS




Paper, vellum, and translucence
These materials communicate memory, intimacy, and partial knowledge.


    The archive is not a hard database. It is layered. Vellum lets the
    user feel that memory is partly visible, partly covered, and slowly
    understood. Paper gives the system human scale. Use it in empty
    states, memory cards, archive covers, onboarding profile cards,
    and marketing imagery.




    DIGITAL RULES



    Low opacity textures only. Use them as masked backgrounds, not tiled
    wallpaper. Add texture at 4-10% opacity unless it is the hero visual. Keep
    text on clean surfaces.

Watercolor language
Watercolor is the atmosphere of understanding, not the brand itself.

    • Use watercolor to indicate meaning forming out of memory: insights,
      reflections, selected archive covers.
    • Prefer pale blue washes with organic edges and paper grain.
    • Avoid rainbow watercolor, paint splashes, or art-school expressionism.
    • Do not put body text over strong texture. Use a clean white or cloud overlay
      when text must sit nearby.
    • Watercolor can animate subtly as a slow reveal or diffusion, but should
      never loop distractingly.

IMAGERY




Photography direction
The camera should make Kyo feel tangible before the product is fully tangible.

    • Shoot real materials: translucent paper, frosted acrylic, aluminum edges, binder
      rings, paper grain, water droplets.
    • Lighting should be cool, soft, and directional. Avoid warm sunset lifestyle
      photography.
    • Composition should be quiet and spacious. Let small blue details carry
      attention.
    • Depth of field can be shallow in material shots, but hero compositions should
      remain clean and legible.
    • Human imagery should be rare, documentary, and provenance-based - not
      stock wellness photography.

LAYOUT




Layout principles
The layout should feel archived, not assembled.

    • Use editorial compositions with large negative space and one calm
      focal object.
    • Avoid dense dashboard grids. Kyo is not analytics software.
    • Separate groups with hairline rules, not heavy cards.
    • Use asymmetry when showing memory or archive; use symmetry
      when showing trust and setup.
    • Let images sit as objects on the page, with subtle shadows and soft
      edges.

Elevation and depth
Depth should resemble layered materials, not app-card shadows.

    • Use subtle shadows with large blur and low opacity, as if paper is floating just
      above stone.
    • Use 1px borders more often than shadows in dense UI.
    • Elevated cards should rise 2-8px visually; modal surfaces can rise 12-20px.
    • Use inner highlights for aluminum or frosted surfaces, but never make them
      glossy.
    • Avoid heavy drop shadows, dark glows, glassmorphism excess, and
      neumorphic buttons.

MOTION




Motion system
Motion should feel like attention arriving, not UI showing off.

    • Default duration: 180-240ms for controls, 500-900ms for meaning reveals.
    • Easing: soft ease-out; avoid elastic or playful bounces.
    • Capture reaction: slight material lift, then text appears with a calm fade.
    • Graph connection: node fades first, line draws slowly second, label arrives last.
    • Archive open: card expands like a page turning or vellum layer lifting, but never literal
      gimmickry.
    • Reduced motion: replace movement with opacity transitions and state changes.

Haptics and sound

HANDOFF




Quality bar before build handoff
The design system should be judged by feel, not just screenshots.

    •   Does the interface still feel calm after real user data is inside it?
    •   Are there at least three white layers before blue enters the scene?
    •   Can a user understand what is interactive without loud colors?
    •   Do texture and typography support meaning, or are they decorative?
    •   Would this still feel premium if all imagery were removed?
    •   Does the UI avoid looking like a chatbot, dashboard, therapy app, or habit tracker?

GUARDRAILS




What not to do
The wrong move turns Kyo into a category the product is explicitly not in.

    • Do not make it look like Notion, Arc, Raycast, or a generic AI dashboard.
    • Do not use noisy gradients, rainbow AI glows, sparkles, or black-mode sci-fi
      treatments.
    • Do not overuse cards, pills, badges, or dashboard modules in the marketing page.
    • Do not let the serif become decorative. It should carry meaning, not vibe.
    • Do not overdo the Japanese cues. The name has meaning; the brand should not
      become aesthetic cosplay.
    • Do not use warm lifestyle imagery, wellness stock photos, or therapy-app pastel
      palettes.
